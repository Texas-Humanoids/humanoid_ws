The pipeline

Everything flows one way, from the CAD export to the motors. Each box is one file or script, and each arrow is a file the next stage reads. The deploy contract is policy_spec.json: the bridge on the Jetson builds its observation and scales its actions from that file alone, so the robot can't silently disagree with training.

Training and both sweeps run on the Windows training PC. The robot side runs in the robot Docker container on the Jetson. The bring-up tools run on the Jetson host with raw SocketCAN, no ROS.

The tuning loop

Change one thing, run the pipeline, read the scorecard. The sweeps are deterministic, so a change in the scorecard is caused by your change, not by noise.

# 1. edit a knob (tables below), usually in robots/walker_demo.py or tasks/walker_demo_velocity/env_cfg.py
# 2. one command: train (~40 min on the 5060 Ti) + package + MuJoCo sweep + Isaac sweep + scorecard (~2 min)
humanoid_ws/training/scripts/demo_pipeline.sh Cad cad_v2
# re-check an existing run without training:
SKIP_TRAIN=1 RUN_NAME=v1 humanoid_ws/training/scripts/demo_pipeline.sh Cad cad_v1
# 3. read humanoid_ws_sysid/policies/walker_demo/SCORECARD.md

What the sweeps do

  • 46 s script: stand, forward 0.2 and 0.4, stop, back, sideways both ways, turn both ways, forward + turn, stick drift, stand.
  • Four 0.25-0.3 m/s pushes during it.
  • 10 conditions in MuJoCo, 11 in Isaac: soft and stiff gains, 15 and 25 ms delay, friction 0.4, weak and strong motors, a 30 V pack, and all of them at once.

Decision rule

  • Any fall in any condition rules a policy out, however good its tracking.
  • Then prefer better Isaac tracking (PhysX is the stricter simulator here).
  • Then lower ankle peak and hip-roll RMS torque (heat).
  • Then check used_ranges.json fits inside the robot's measured hard stops.

The cad_safe_v1 lesson. It trained on slow commands only, with no sideways at all, plus wider randomization and gentler-motion penalties. It came out less robust: all four falls happened when the test asked for a small sideways step it had never seen. A policy is only reliable inside what it trained on. Keep a small range of every command in training, and cap speeds on the gamepad instead.

None of the policies step sideways (vy tracks about 0), so the demo drives with forward/back and turning. Both cad and wide use a small slice of the joint range: hip pitch -0.47 to +0.05, knee 0.26-0.93, ankle -0.56 to +0.04, hip roll within ~0.1 of 0 (used_ranges.json).

Rules that keep sim and robot in step

Needs a retrain if changed

  • PD gains (kp/kd)
  • Default pose
  • Action scale
  • Gait period
  • Observation terms, order or scales
  • Joint limits the policy was trained with

Safe to change on the robot

  • Gamepad speeds (gamepad_demo.yaml)
  • Yaml clamp limits, as long as they cover used_ranges.json
  • IMU mounting rotation
  • Heading-hold gain
  • Motor IDs, CAN adapters, directions, offsets
Operating rule for the demo. To stop walking, let go of LB: the command goes to zero and it stands. BACK disarms, the motors damp and the robot drops, so use it only in an emergency, with the gantry catching. The open risk the policy can't fix is the ~820 ms robstride_motor_node stall seen on the bench. Keep the gantry on until it's root-caused.
